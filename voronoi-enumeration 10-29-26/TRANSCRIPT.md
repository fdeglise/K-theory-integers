## 6. Assistant

I have a concrete proposal: **enumerate low-dimensional cells directly using bounded-index minimal-vector frames and faces of Ryshkov polytopes.** It gives a completeness argument; I have not implemented or benchmarked it.

The useful reduction is that the missing computations identified for \(K_{12}(\mathbb Z)\) are
\[
H_{12}\!\left(\operatorname{Vor}_{\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb Z)}\right),
\qquad n=9,10,11.
\]
Thus we need projective cells of dimensions \(11,12,13\), corresponding to cones of dimensions \(12,13,14\). See [Dutour Sikirić–Elbaz-Vincent–Kupers–Martinet, Remark 6.6](https://jamartin.perso.math.cnrs.fr/Publications/DEKM.pdf).

Here is the algorithm I would pursue.

1. **Enumerate bounded-index frames.**

   Every relevant cell is generated by minimal vectors spanning \(\mathbb R^n\). Choose \(n\) independent ones and use them as coordinate vectors \(e_1,\ldots,e_n\). The ambient lattice then becomes a superlattice
   \[
   \mathbb Z^n\subseteq L\subset\mathbb Q^n.
   \]

   If \(H_n\) is a certified upper bound for the Hermite constant, its index satisfies
   \[
   I=[L:\mathbb Z^n]\le D_n:=\left\lfloor H_n^{\,n/2}\right\rfloor.
   \]
   This follows by normalizing the minimum to \(1\), then combining Hadamard’s inequality for the frame with the Hermite bound.

   Enumerate these superlattices by Hermite normal form, reducing by signed coordinate permutations. **Allow \(I>1\): independent minimal vectors need not form a lattice basis.**

2. **For each frame, construct its polytope of possible forms.**

   In these coordinates, consider
   \[
   P_L=\left\{G=G^t:\ G_{ii}=1,\quad
   x^tGx\ge1\text{ for every }0\ne x\in L\right\}.
   \]
   Its points are exactly the forms for which the chosen frame consists of minimal vectors.

   This is a bounded rational polytope contained in the positive-definite cone, when nonempty. In particular, \(e_i\pm e_j\) give \(|G_{ij}|\le\tfrac12\). The relevant finiteness theory is explained in [Schürmann’s treatment of Ryshkov polyhedra](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0901.1587).

   There is also an explicit finite candidate set:
   \[
   C_L=\left\{0\ne x\in L:\ |x_i|\le D_n/I\ \text{for every }i\right\}/\{\pm1\}.
   \]
   Every vector that can be minimal anywhere in \(P_L\) belongs to \(C_L\). Indeed, replacing frame vector \(e_i\) by a minimal vector \(x\) produces, when independent, a minimal-vector frame of index \(I|x_i|\le D_n\).

   This supplies a finite exhaustive fallback. In practice I would generate constraints lazily using linear programming and exact shortest-vector computations.

3. **Enumerate only the required faces.**

   For a face \(F\subseteq P_L\), choose a rational form in its relative interior and obtain its complete minimal-vector set \(V_F\). The associated Voronoi cone is
   \[
   \sigma_F=\operatorname{cone}\{xx^t:x\in V_F\}.
   \]
   With \(m=n(n+1)/2\),
   \[
   \dim\sigma_F=m-\dim F.
   \]

   Therefore search only faces satisfying \(\dim F\ge m-14\). Starting from \(P_L\), this requires at most:

   | Lattice rank | Face-search depth |
   |---|---:|
   | \(9\) | \(5\) |
   | \(10\) | \(4\) |
   | \(11\) | \(3\) |

   Use complete active sets and exact ranks. **Do not assume simpliciality:** a cone’s dimension is the rank of its matrices \(xx^t\), which can be smaller than its number of rays.

4. **Identify orbits and build the differential.**

   Convert each configuration back to integral lattice coordinates and identify duplicates under \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb Z)\), retaining explicit equivalence matrices. Compute stabilizers and their orientation characters, then signed facet incidences to obtain
   \[
   C_{13}\xrightarrow{\partial_{13}}C_{12}
   \xrightarrow{\partial_{12}}C_{11}.
   \]
   Check \(\partial_{12}\partial_{13}=0\) and compute kernel modulo image exactly. The orientation and incidence conventions are given in [Elbaz-Vincent–Gangl–Soulé, §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.0789).

The completeness argument is that every relevant cell contains an independent minimal-vector frame; that frame occurs in the bounded-index catalogue; and its minimal-vector configuration occurs on a face of the corresponding \(P_L\).

The substantial uncertainty is **tractability**, particularly the rank-\(11\) frame catalogue and the size of the candidate sets. I would begin by reproducing known lower-dimensional enumerations, then measure these two costs. For the eventual \(K\)-theory argument, computing over \(\mathbb Z[1/2310]\) handles the stabilizer-prime exclusions for these ranks; the Hurewicz and small-prime arguments still need to be attached to the computed homology.

## 17. User

## 18. Assistant

**Rank \(4\) tests the mathematical machinery; rank \(11\) requires a substantially different search engine.** The target is shallow in cone dimension, but still takes place in a large space of forms.

| Aspect | Rank \(4\) | Rank \(11\) target |
|---|---|---|
| Symmetric-matrix coordinates | 10 | 66 |
| Free coordinates after fixing a minimal frame | 6 | 55 |
| Cone dimensions sought | Up to 10 | 12–14 |
| Additional independent rank-one constraints beyond the frame | Up to 6 | At most 3 |

That last row is our main advantage. We should exploit it instead of constructing entire slice polytopes.

The missing complexity is principally:

1. **A complete, manageable frame catalogue.** Rank \(4\) has an index bound of \(2\). In rank \(11\), enumerating all bounded-index superlattices naively becomes enormous. Even index \(p\), for prime \(p\), gives \((p^{11}-1)/(p-1)\) models before symmetry reduction. We need classification under symmetry and rigorous pruning of impossible frames. The rank-\(11\) catalogue in the [published computation](https://jamartin.perso.math.cnrs.fr/Publications/DEKM.pdf) is explicitly incomplete.

2. **Face closure without enumerating vertices.** Enumerating all vertices would recreate much of the perfect-form classification problem. We need an exact LP/SVP oracle that determines feasibility, forced minimal vectors, and a relative-interior witness, while exploring only configurations of rank at most \(14\).

3. **Generating extensions without scanning huge vector boxes.** The finite candidate bounds prove termination, but are too expensive as literal loops. We need symmetry orbits, maximal-index-frame bounds, and incremental constraint generation to produce candidates efficiently.

4. **Scalable integral equivalence and stabilizers.** The prototype tries signed ordered frames. That becomes prohibitive in rank \(11\). We need lattice-aware canonicalization, strong invariants, and efficient automorphism computations that preserve the ambient lattice.

5. **A search that can finish across many machines.** Checkpointing, duplicate suppression across frames, task distribution, and independently checkable certificates become essential.

6. **The homology computation itself.** Enumeration must retain incidence maps and orientation data, then assemble and process potentially large sparse boundary matrices. Counts of cell orbits alone cannot establish the required vanishing.

So an \(n=4\) implementation would validate exact closure, nonsimplicial cells, and equivalence handling. It would give little evidence that the **frame classification and extension search** can finish for \(n=11\). Those are the central research and engineering problems still ahead.

Two of these have particularly concrete solutions.

**For closure**, we impose the proposed minimal-vector equalities, solve a rational LP, and search for lattice vectors violating its inequalities. Added inequalities refine the problem. Determining the face’s affine hull and taking a relative-interior point gives its complete forced minimal-vector set. Our finite candidate bound supplies an exhaustive fallback; the practical question is how often that fallback is needed.

**For equivalence**, a configuration \(S\) supplies an intrinsic positive-definite matrix
\[
C_S=\sum_{v\in S/\{\pm1\}}vv^t.
\]
An integral equivalence \(U:S\to T\) necessarily satisfies
\[
U C_S U^t=C_T.
\]
Thus lattice-isometry algorithms can generate candidates, which we check against the actual vector configurations. This replaces the prototype’s enumeration of signed ordered frames.

The honest gap is **search size**. I can specify a mathematically complete algorithm, but I do not yet know how to make the frame catalogue and extension generation small enough for rank \(11\). I would investigate those two bottlenecks before investing heavily in distributed execution or the homology backend.
